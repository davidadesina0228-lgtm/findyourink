If Jesus Is God, Why Did He Pray to God?

By Angel Kanu — July 27, 2026

Key Takeaways

  • Jesus kneeling to pray has nothing to do with Islam — and His calling God "Father" actually contradicts Islamic theology, where such language is considered Shirk.
  • Jesus is the eternal Word, who was both with God and was God (John 1:1) — one divine being in three distinct persons, not three separate gods.
  • The "Helper" Jesus promised in John 14:16 is the Holy Spirit — "another of the same kind" — not a future prophet; Mohammed's death disqualifies him from Jesus’s promise never to leave His people.
  • At the incarnation, Jesus emptied Himself of the outward expression of divinity (Philippians 2:5-8) and lived as a man dependent on the Holy Spirit — which is why He prayed, asked "who touched me?", and called God "Father."
  • Scripture repeatedly shows people — disciples, a Samaritan woman, even demons — recognizing Jesus as the Christ, the Son of God, while He walked the earth as a man.

Frequently Asked Questions

If Jesus is God, why did He pray to the Father?

At the incarnation, Jesus "emptied Himself" of the outward expression of divine equality (Philippians 2:5-8) and lived as a man dependent on the Holy Spirit. Prayer expressed the Father-Son relationship He modeled for believers — not an admission that He was not God.

Does Jesus praying and kneeling mean He was following Islamic practice?

No. Kneeling to pray isn't unique to any one religion, and Jesus calling God "Father" (Luke 22:42) actually contradicts Islamic theology, where ascribing "Father" to Allah is considered Shirk — associating a human quality with God.

Is the “Helper” Jesus promised in John 14 a reference to Mohammed?

No. John 14:16-18 explicitly identifies the Helper as the Holy Spirit, using the Greek allos parakletos — "another of the same kind," meaning another of the same divine nature as Jesus. Jesus also promised "I will not leave you orphans," a promise the prophet Mohammed's death could not fulfill.

Does the Trinity mean Christians worship three gods?

No. Christians worship one God who exists in three distinct persons — one being, three persons, not three separate beings. This differs from three humans (three beings, three persons), but is not a contradiction, since God's nature is not bound by human limitation.
